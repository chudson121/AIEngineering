# AI Operational Framework

1. **Understand the Fundamentals**: Begin by understanding the fundamental workings of generative AI - how it's trained, how it generates outputs, and its limitations. This understanding will form the basis of all strategies.
    
2. **Question Assumptions**: Challenge traditional ways of thinking or established norms about AI. For instance, instead of assuming AI will always lead to job losses, consider how it could create new roles or improve existing ones.
    
3. **Innovate**: Look for innovative solutions to mitigate risks. For example, could an AI system be built to detect and correct its own hallucinations or biases?
    
4. **Simplicity**: Focus on simple, efficient solutions. For instance, instead of complex regulations, could a simple code of ethics guide AI usage?
    
5. **Experimentation**: Be open to trial and error. Test different strategies and learn from the outcomes. If one approach to data privacy doesn't work, try another.
    
6. **Optimization**: Always look for ways to improve and optimize. For example, continually evaluate the performance of AI systems and look for ways to increase accuracy or reduce bias.
    
7. **Ethics**: Prioritize ethical considerations from the ground up, not just as an afterthought. This includes fairness, transparency, privacy, and accountability.
    
8. **Collaboration**: Work closely with AI vendors, regulatory bodies, employees, and other stakeholders. Collaboration can lead to better understanding and more effective strategies.

## **Privacy-Centric Business Model**: Intellectual Property Rights
Examining their business model is crucial when considering a secure and private generative AI provider. A trustworthy provider operates with a clear and transparent business model, where the sole purpose of your data is to offer you their service and nothing else. 
- Their revenue should come exclusively from selling subscriptions and services. In particular, it is essential to rule out any additional money-making methods, such as selling, renting, or sharing your data with third parties for advertising or marketing purposes. Ensuring that privacy guarantees are fundamental and accessible to all users, regardless of their subscription level, is paramount. 
- Ensure the provider uses de-identification techniques to sanitize the data collected from sensitive and personally identifiable information (e.g., social security numbers, credit card numbers, and email addresses). 
- Identify your providers and any sub processors they leverage, including which LLMs they use and for what purpose. You should ensure that the sub processors processing your data also comply with the same security expectations. 
- Does your provider and any sub processors have CISO's, dedicated security organizations, or public bug bounty programs? Were there any publicly known breaches, and how did they respond? What admin controls (SSO, Role based permissions) do they provide? What do their security and compliance roadmaps look like?
- Ensure the provider has no claims of ownership rights under any circumstances. The provider cannot have the ability to sell your data. They should not have the right to assign access privileges to other parties.

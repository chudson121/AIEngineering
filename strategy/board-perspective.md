# AI - Board perspective

A Strategic Guide for Board of Directors: Integrating Artificial Intelligence into Your Organization

As the pace of technological innovation accelerates, Artificial Intelligence (AI) is becoming an essential tool for businesses in various industries. For board members seeking to integrate AI into their organizations, it's crucial to understand the potential benefits, risks, and strategic considerations that come with this powerful technology.

**Understanding the AI Landscape**

AI has the potential to transform many aspects of business, from improving operational efficiency to delivering personalized customer experiences. However, it also brings new risks and challenges. The first step in successfully integrating AI is building AI literacy among board members. This can be achieved through learning resources, expert lectures, or even using AI tools like language models to grasp the complexities of AI technology.

**Promoting AI Fluency in the C-Suite**

While board members need a solid understanding of AI, it's even more critical for executives to be fluent in AI technology. As the decision-makers who shape AI programs, executives must understand the value, opportunities, and risks of AI. Encouraging AI fluency in the C-suite can help the organization use AI responsibly and effectively.

**Expanding the Board's Expertise**

The complexity of AI might necessitate expanding the board's expertise. Consider recruiting board members with operational AI experience who can provide valuable insights for AI governance and oversight.

**AI Governance and Future Orientation**

As AI technology evolves, the board must stay ahead of the curve. Establishing a dedicated subcommittee to focus on AI can ensure the board remains informed about this fast-changing technology. This group can guide the ethical and trustworthy use of AI, aligning with the organization's overall strategy.

**Transparency and Ethical Considerations**

Promoting transparency about the organization's use of AI is crucial. This includes clear communication about the types of AI in use, their purposes, and the measures to mitigate risks. The board should also address ethical considerations of AI, such as privacy, bias, and fairness issues.

**Understanding the Regulatory Landscape**

The board should stay informed about current and potential future AI regulations to anticipate and mitigate legal and compliance issues.

**Continuous Learning and Adaptation**

AI is a rapidly evolving field. The board must stay updated on the latest advancements and understand how they can impact the organization. This requires a commitment to continuous learning and adaptation.

**Measuring AI Success**

Establishing metrics to measure the success of AI initiatives can help the board monitor progress and make informed decisions. These metrics could be financial returns, efficiency improvements, or customer satisfaction levels.

**Data Management**

AI systems often rely on large amounts of data. The board should understand the sources of this data, how it's being used, and how it's being protected to ensure data security and privacy.

**Shaping the AI Strategy**

Finally, the board plays a crucial role in shaping the company's overall AI strategy. This strategy should align with the company's broader goals and outline how AI can help achieve them.

Integrating AI into an organization is a strategic process that requires careful planning and oversight. By building AI literacy, promoting AI fluency among executives, expanding the board's expertise, and staying informed about AI developments and regulations, boards of directors can successfully guide their organizations into the new era of AI.

---
### Build the board’s AI literacy

Being an advocate and guide for AI risk management means, in part, asking the right questions. This necessitates a level of AI literacy, which was already important. With Generative AI, the need for literacy is even more paramount, as the technology presents new types of risk (e.g., “hallucinated” outputs that are factually false), as well as magnifies existing risks due to scale (e.g., a Generative AI-enabled call center could give biased outputs to a greater number of people).

To take part in Generative AI risk management, board members can build AI literacy through traditional methods, such as bringing in speakers and subject matter experts and pursuing independent learning through classes, lectures, and reading. A Generative AI model like an LLM could also help in this regard, as simple prompts can summarize and help explain, in natural language, the complexities of how Generative AI works, its limits, and its capabilities.

### Promote AI fluency in the C-suite

If Generative AI literacy in the boardroom is important, fluency in the C-suite is even more so. Board members are in a position to urge executives to build Generative AI fluency around not only the value and opportunities, but the risks as well. As the power and allure of Generative AI grows and use cases multiply, business leaders need knowledge and familiarity with the technology to responsibly shape AI programs. Decisions around AI ethics, safety and security, accountability, and all the factors that impact trust in AI flow out of a baseline understanding of what Generative AI is and what it can do. As stewards of the enterprise, board members can encourage the AI fluency that is more important than it has ever been.

### Consider recruiting board members with AI experience

Board members often hail from fields steeped in finance and business management. This background allows them to be informed leaders on fiscal and competitiveness issues. Given that AI is a technical and complex field raising its own collection of hurdles and risks, boards may look to expand their in-house subject matter expertise by recruiting an AI professional to the board. Such a person should bring experience as an operational AI leader with a track record of implementing successful AI projects in similar organizations.

While Generative AI is relatively new and some of the earliest use cases are only now being deployed, a professional with operational AI experience can provide the insight boards need for oversight and governance.

### Orienting the board for the future

Governance is not an ad hoc exercise, and boards face the need to implement controls to guide the ethical and trustworthy use of Generative AI. Boards may stand up subcommittees to oversee vital enterprise activities, such as for audits, succession planning, and risk management related to finance and operations. Generative AI governance can be supported with a similar tactic.

The lexicon, capabilities, risks, and trajectory of Generative AI are all in flux as the technology matures. A subcommittee or dedicated group is positioned to remain focused and informed on this complex, fast-changing technology. Boards could also extend existing subcommittees mandate to include Generative AI components, for example the Audit committee can include planning for algorithmic auditing.

### Guide the organization as Generative AI matures

Given their role, board members are not directly working with Generative AI, but they are important stakeholders with essential responsibilities. As enterprise leadership and lines of business explore how Generative AI can be a productivity enhancer and innovation driver, the board can take a higher level, big picture view of AI programs and focus on guiding the enterprise in the ethical and trustworthy deployment of Generative AI. In this, it is helpful to leverage a framework for assessing risk and trust and how those impact compliance and governance Responsible AI frameworks are suited to assessing risk and trust in any AI deployment, and it can help board members make clear-eyed evaluations and usher the organization toward the most valuable use of Generative AI.

The Generative AI landscape is still nascent. These are the early days of a new technology that will have profound impacts on business and society. As well as taking these five important steps, board members can turn to advisors who are already developing the tactics and standards for Generative AI governance and oversight.

While risk management is always a moving target, with literacy, professional experience, dedicated attention, and a vision for the future, boards are positioned to guide their organizations into this new era of AI.


1. **Transparency and Communication**: It is essential for the board of directors to promote transparency about the organization's use of AI, both internally and externally. This includes communicating about the types of AI in use, their purposes, and the measures taken to mitigate risks.
    
2. **Ethical Considerations**: The ethical considerations of AI should be a significant part of the board’s discussion. This includes issues related to privacy, bias, and fairness. The board should consider establishing an AI ethics committee or similar body.
    
3. **Regulatory Landscape**: Understanding the current and potential future regulatory landscape of AI is crucial. This can help the board to anticipate possible legal and compliance issues.
    
4. **Continuous Learning and Adaptation**: AI is a rapidly evolving field. It’s important for the board to stay updated on the latest advancements and understand how they can impact the organization. This can be achieved through regular training sessions, attending AI-focused seminars or conferences, etc.
    
5. **Measuring AI Success**: Establish metrics to measure the success of AI initiatives. This could be in terms of financial returns, improved efficiency, customer satisfaction, or other relevant indicators.
    
6. **Data Management**: AI systems often rely on large amounts of data. Boards need to understand where data is coming from, how it's being used, and how it's being protected.
    
7. **AI Strategy**: Lastly, the board should help shape the company's overall AI strategy. This strategy should align with the company's broader goals and should outline how AI can help achieve them.
    

Remember, the board's role in AI governance isn't just about managing risk—it's also about identifying and capitalizing on opportunities.

2. Opportunities and Threats of AI and Advanced Analytics:
    - Business Value: Increased revenue streams and market share, enhancing the company's competitive position.
    - OKR: Identify 5 new business opportunities through AI by Q2.
    - KPI: Number of new business opportunities identified through AI.
    - Questions:
        - What are the opportunities to use these technologies to improve existing processes and generate new business opportunities?
        - Does pursuing AI or advanced analytics pose any threats for the company?
        - What is the risk of not leveraging AI within the company or for some specific function?
        - How might the competitive environment change with the adoption of Generative AI?

    - Business Value: Reduced potential losses, maintained reputation, and a more stable environment for growth by managing risks effectively.
    - OKR: Mitigate 90% of identified AI-related risks by Q3.
    - KPI: Number of AI-related risks mitigated.
    - Questions:
        - Do we have the necessary capabilities?
        - How will generative AI affect our industry and company in the short and longer term?
        - Are we balancing value creation with adequate risk management?

    - Business Value: Better resource allocation and a higher likelihood of achieving strategic objectives through AI alignment.
    - OKR: Achieve 100% alignment between AI adoption and 3 key corporate strategic objectives by Q4.
    - KPI: Degree of alignment between AI adoption and corporate strategic objectives.
    - Questions:
        - How does the adoption of generative AI align with our corporate vision, mission, and strategic objectives?
        - How can generative AI help us achieve our strategic goals and create a competitive advantage?
        - How will the adoption of generative AI shape our business model in the next 5-10 years?
        - Are we prepared for the potential disruptive effects of generative AI on our workforce and operations?
5. Market and Industry Analysis:
    - Business Value: Increased market share and profitability through gained competitive advantages.
    - OKR: Gain 3 competitive advantages through AI by Q2.
    - KPI: Number of competitive advantages gained through AI.

        - How is generative AI disrupting our industry and how can we leverage it to stay ahead of our competition?
        - How do our customers and stakeholders perceive the use of generative AI, and how can we address any concerns?
6. Investment, Resource Allocation, and Financial Implications:
    - Business Value: Justified AI investment contributing to the company's financial success.
    - OKR: Achieve 20% ROI from AI adoption by Q3.
    - KPI: Return on investment (ROI) from AI adoption.

        - What level of investment is required to adopt generative AI and how will this impact our overall budget and resource allocation?
        - Can we leverage partnerships or collaborations to share the costs and risks associated with generative AI adoption?
        - What is the projected cost of implementing and maintaining generative AI systems in our enterprise?
        - What kind of return on investment can we expect from the adoption of generative AI?

    - Business Value: Reduced impact of AI-related issues, potentially saving time, money, and reputational damage.
